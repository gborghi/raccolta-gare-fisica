P1. Accelerometer.

An accelerometer is, as its name suggests, a device for measuring accelerations. A simple model consists of a cylinder whose axis coincides with the direction of acceleration to be measured. Inside the cylinder, there is a ball attached at both ends by two identical springs and a viscous liquid that dampens oscillations of the ball when acceleration changes. In the absence of acceleration, the ball is in equilibrium at the center of the cylinder, as shown in Figure 1a. However, when the device undergoes acceleration along the OX direction indicated, the ball reaches a new dynamic equilibrium state displaced by a distance
0 < x,
as indicated in Figure 1b. This displacement remains constant as long as the acceleration is constant.

The accelerometer is mounted horizontally in a vehicle such that the cylinder’s axis aligns with the direction of motion (OX axis). Under these conditions, determine the equilibrium displacement of the ball, x, as a function of the vehicle’s acceleration, a, the spring constant of each spring, k, and the ball’s mass, M.
