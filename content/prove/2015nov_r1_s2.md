Electrons are accelerated through a potential $V_0$, producing a horizontal beam along the $x$-axis. The beam enters a perfectly uniform electric field parallel to the $y$-axis, produced by a parallel plate capacitor with potential difference $V_P$ between the plates, which have separation $d$ and are of length $L$, as shown in Figure 2.1.

(a) Determine an expression for:

(i) the initial velocity $v_0$ of the electrons entering the electric field.

(ii) the deflection angle $\alpha$ of the beam emerging from the electric field in terms of $V_0$ and $V_P$.

(iii) the displacement at P, $y$, of the emerging beam from the $x$-axis in terms of $V_P$.

[8]

(b) Calculate the transition time $t$ of an electron through the electric field, given that $V_0 = 2{,}000$ V, $V_P = 50.0$ V, $L = 4.00$ cm and $d = 1.50$ cm.

[2]

(c) On emerging from the parallel plates, the beam enters an identical electrostatic field that is at right angles to the initial field, perpendicular to the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis, along the $z$-axis. Determine an algebraic expression for the final speed of an electron exiting the second field and the angles it makes with the Cartesian coordinate axes.

A vertical glass tube, of height $2L$, is sealed at its lower end and open at the top end to the atmosphere. It contains $n$ moles of air in the lower half (i.e. length $L$) of volume $V_0$, and mercury of density $\rho$ in the top half. As the air expands the mercury is expelled. Atmospheric pressure $P_0$ corresponds to length $L$ of mercury in a barometer.

(a) Air expands, occupying an additional length $l$ of the tube, but with total volume $V$ and pressure $P$. Using the reduced volume $\tilde{V} = V / V_0$ and the reduced pressure $\tilde{P} = P / P_0$ of the air, express these quantities in terms of the reduced value of $l$, $\tilde{l} = l / L$. Deduce:

(i) the relation between $\tilde{P}$ and $\tilde{V}$ that is independent of temperature.

(ii) the general ideal gas law, for the air in the tube at temperature $T$, in terms of the reduced variables $\tilde{P}$, $\tilde{V}$ and $\tilde{T}$ only, where $\tilde{T} = T / T_0$ and $T_0 = P_0 V_0 / (nR)$.

[4]

(b) Sketch on a $\tilde{P} - \tilde{V}$ graph for the system:

(i) the behaviour of the air in the physically admissible region.

(ii) the isotherms for the air, indicating which are at the higher temperatures by labelling using $\tilde{T}_1 > \tilde{T}_2 > \tilde{T}_3 > $ etc.

Comment on the symmetry of the curves in (i) and (ii). Deduce the highest temperature reached by the air in the tube.

[8]

(c) When a small change in the reduced temperature $\Delta\tilde{T}$ and reduced volume $\Delta\tilde{V}$ takes place

$$\frac{5\Delta\tilde{T}}{2} = \tilde{C}\,\Delta\tilde{T} - \tilde{P}\,\Delta\tilde{V}$$

where $\tilde{C}$ is the specific heat per mole of the air in the tube. Deduce that

$$\tilde{C} = \frac{21 - 12\tilde{V}}{6 - 4\tilde{V}}$$

[4]

(d) Sketch a graph of $\tilde{C}$ against $\tilde{V}$ in the physically admissible region.

[4]

La pressione atmosferica $P_0$ corrisponde alla lunghezza $L$ del mercurio in un barometro.

a) L'aria si espandono, occupando una lunghezza aggiuntiva $l$ del tubo, ma con volume totale $V$ e pressione $P$. Usando il volume ridotto $\tilde{V} = V / V_0$ e la pressione ridotta $\tilde{P} = P / P_0$ dell'aria, esprimere queste quantità in termini di valore ridotto di $l$, $\tilde{l} = l / L$. Riduzione:
