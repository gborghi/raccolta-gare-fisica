The expansion of a metal rod varies linearly with temperature, in the form of

$$\ell = \ell_0(1 + \alpha \Delta T)$$

where $\ell_0$ and $\ell$ are the initial and final lengths respectively, $\Delta T$ is the temperature change, and $\alpha$ is the coefficient of linear expansion.

An iron rod is $1.00\ \text{m}$ at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$. What is the length of a copper rod at $0\ ^\circ\text{C}$ if the difference between its length and that of the iron rod is not to vary with temperature?

Coefficient of expansion of copper, $\alpha_\text{Cu} = 17.0 \times 10^{-6}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$

Coefficient of expansion of iron, $\alpha_\text{Fe} = 11.9 \times 10^{-6}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$
