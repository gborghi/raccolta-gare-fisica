Help:
Neglecting friction and interactions with other bodies, the total mechanical energy of the meteoroid is conserved: the system meteoroid + Earth constitutes a conservative system if only gravitational force is considered, which implies:
$$E_m = E_c + E_p = \text{constante},\quad (1)$$, where $E_m$, $E_c$ and $E_p$ are the mechanical energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy of the meteoroid, respectively.
Additionally, since Earth's gravitational force is a central force (directed along the line connecting the meteoroid to the center of Earth), the angular momentum of the meteoroid ($L$) is conserved along its trajectory. The angular momentum is defined as $L = r \times p$, where $r$ is the position vector of the meteoroid relative to Earth's center and $p = mv$ is its linear momentum ($v$ being its velocity vector). Una esfera metálica aislada de radio $R_1 = 9\ \text{cm}$, se carga a un potencial de $V_{1,0} = 10^4\ \text{V}$, como se
muestra en la figura 4(a). A continuación, se toca con otra esfera metálica aislada y descargada,
de radio $R_2 = 3\ \text{cm}$, como se indica en la figura 4(b). Después la segunda esfera se descarga. El
proceso de puesta en contacto de ambas esferas previa descarga de la segunda se repite n veces,
como se indica en la figura 4(c).
a) [0.5 puntos] Calcular la carga inicial de la primera esfera, $Q_{1,0}$, estando a un potencial $V_{1,0} = 10^4$
V, antes de ponerse en contacto con la segunda esfera.
b) [1 punto] Calcular las cargas, $Q_{1,1}$ y $Q_{2,1}$, y los potenciales, $V_{1,1}$ y $V_{2,1}$, de ambas esferas después
de la primera puesta en contacto, antes de la descarga de la segunda. ¿Se verifica la conservación
de la carga?
c) [1 punto] Obtener las expresiones para la carga y el potencial de la primera esfera al cabo de n
descargas ($Q_{1,n}$ y $V_{1,n}$), en función de la carga inicial $Q_{1,0}$, de los radios $R_1$, $R_2$ y de n.
Ayuda:
La carga de una esfera metálica aislada de radio $R$ a potencial $V$ es $Q = 4\pi\epsilon_0 RV$. Equivalentemente, el potencial de una esfera metálica aislada de radio $R$ con carga $Q$ es $V = Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0 R)$.
Al poner en contacto dos esferas conductoras: 1) se redistribuye la carga entre ellas, de manera
que ambas se sitúan al mismo potencial y 2) la carga total se conserva (la suma de las cargas de
las esferas antes y después de la puesta en contacto es la misma).
Dato:
Permitividad dieléctrica del vacío: $\epsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\ \text{C}^2 \cdot \text{N}^{-1} \cdot \text{m}^{-2}$.
Figura 4: (a) Al ponerse a un potencial $V_{1,0}$, una esfera metálica aislada de radio $R_1$
adquiere una carga $Q_{1,0}$. (b) Al ser tocada por otra esfera metálica aislada y descargada
de radio $R_2$, los potenciales de ambas esferas se igualan ($V_{1,1} = V_{2,1}$), repartiéndose la
carga $Q_{1,0}$ entre ambas ($Q_{1,0} = Q_{1,1} + Q_{2,1}$, con $Q_{1,1} \neq Q_{2,1}$ si $R_1 \neq R_2$). A continuación,
la segunda esfera se descarga. (c) El proceso descrito en b) se repite n veces.

PROBLEM 4.
An isolated metallic sphere of radius $R_1 = 9\ \text{cm}$ is charged to a potential of $V_{1,0} = 10^4\ \text{V}$, as shown in Figure 4(a). Subsequently, it is brought into contact with another isolated and uncharged metallic sphere of radius $R_2 = 3\ \text{cm}$, as indicated in Figure 4(b). Then, the second sphere is discharged. The process of contacting both spheres—after discharging the second—is repeated n times, as shown in Figure 4(c).

a) [0.5 points] Calculate the initial charge of the first sphere, $Q_{1,0}$, when it is at a potential $V_{1,0} = 10^4$ V before coming into contact with the second sphere.

b) [1 point] Calculate the charges, $Q_{1,1}$ and $Q_{2,1}$, and the potentials, $V_{1,1}$ and $V_{2,1}$, of both spheres after the first contact, before discharging the second sphere. Does charge conservation hold?

c) [1 point] Derive expressions for the charge and potential of the first sphere after n discharges ($Q_{1,n}$ and $V_{1,n}$), in terms of the initial charge $Q_{1,0}$, the radii $R_1$ and $R_2$, and n.

Hint:
The charge of an isolated metallic sphere of radius $R$ at potential $V$ is $Q = 4\pi\epsilon_0 RV$. Conversely, the potential of an isolated metallic sphere of radius $R$ with charge $Q$ is $V = Q/(4\pi\epsilon_0 R)$.
When two conducting spheres are brought into contact: 1) charge redistributes between them so that both reach the same potential, and 2) total charge is conserved (the sum of charges on the spheres before and after contact remains unchanged).

Data:
Vacuum permittivity: $\epsilon_0 = 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}\ \text{C}^2 \cdot \text{N}^{-1} \cdot \text{m}^{-2}$.
Figure 4: (a) When raised to a potential $V_{1,0}$, an isolated metallic sphere of radius $R_1$ acquires a charge $Q_{1,0}$. (b) When touched by another isolated uncharged metallic sphere of radius $R_2$, the potentials of both spheres become equal ($V_{1,1} = V_{2,1}$), and the charge $Q_{1,0}$ is distributed between them ($Q_{1,0} = Q_{1,1} + Q_{2,1}$, with $Q_{1,1} \neq Q_{2,1}$ if $R_1 \neq R_2$). Subsequently, the second sphere is discharged. (c) The process described in (b) is repeated n times.
