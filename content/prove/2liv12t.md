- Qual è la tensione interna in un punto A del cavo situato $6.43\ \mathrm{m}$ sotto il soffitto?

The engine is running for $t = 48\ \mathrm{s}$ and, at this time, a current $i = 1.02\ \mathrm{A}$ is flowing while the applied voltage is $V = 4.85\ \mathrm{V}$.

How much does the temperature of the electric motor increase as a result of this process?

**NOTE:** Suppose that the only effect of the process is to heat the engine and that heat dissipation can be neglected.

Un satellite cosiddetto "geostazionario" gira intorno alla Terra in (circa) 24 ore, stando ad un'altezza di circa $36\ 000\ \mathrm{km}$ sulla superficie terrestre.

1. In quanto tempo una sonda spaziale percorre un'orbita circolare se la sua altezza dal suolo è di circa $900\ \mathrm{km}$?

Per un esperimento si vuole lanciare, dalla sonda spaziale verso la Terra, un oggetto che cada giù verticalmente, con una velocità iniziale $v_0 = 1.5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ rispetto alla Terra (l'oggetto ha una massa trascurabile rispetto a quella della sonda).

2. Rispetto alla direzione di moto della sonda, a che angolo sarà lanciato l'oggetto?
3. Che velocità viene data all'oggetto, rispetto alla sonda?
4. Supponendo di poter trascurare la presenza dell'atmosfera fino ad una quota di circa $20\ \mathrm{km}$ dal suolo, che velocità ha l'oggetto quando incontra l'atmosfera?

**NOTA:** Nella soluzione del problema utilizzare, oltre ai valori numerici dei dati, solo quelli dell'accelerazione di gravità al suolo $g$ e del raggio della Terra $R_T \approx 6400\ \mathrm{km}$.

A so-called "geostationary" satellite orbits the Earth in (about) 24 hours, standing at a height of about $36\ 000\ \mathrm{km}$ on the Earth's surface.

1. How long does a spacecraft travel in a circular orbit if its altitude from the ground is about $900\ \mathrm{km}$?

For an experiment, you want to launch from the space probe to Earth an object that drops vertically, with an initial speed $v_0 = 1.5\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ relative to the Earth (the object has a negligible mass relative to that of the probe).

2. Compared to the direction of motion of the probe, what angle will the object be launched at?
3. What speed is given to the object, relative to the probe?
4. Assuming you can ignore the presence of the atmosphere up to about $20\ \mathrm{km}$ from the ground, what speed does the object have when it meets the atmosphere?

**NOTE:** In the solution of the problem, in addition to the numerical values of the data, only those of the ground gravity acceleration $g$ and the radius of the Earth $R_T \approx 6400\ \mathrm{km}$ are used.

Consider a container with thermal insulating walls and divided into two rooms by a wall, also thermal insulating, which can flow without friction. In each environment there are initially $n$ moles of a perfect gas mixture at $p_0$ pressure, $T_0$ temperature and $V_0$ volume. An electric heater provides very slow heat to the left-handed environment. The gas expands until the pressure in the right environment reaches $27p_0/8$. The $\gamma$ ratio of the molar specific heat at constant pressure $C_p$ to the molar specific heat at constant volume $C_v$ is $\gamma = C_p/C_v = 3/2$.

Determine as a function of $n$, $R$ and $T_0$:

1. the final temperature of the gas in the environment on the right.
2. The work done on gas in the right environment.
3. the final temperature of the gas in the left environment.
4. How much heat has been absorbed by the gas in the left environment.

4. Since the value of $R_1$ can also be varied, what values should be given to $R_1$ and $R_2$ so that the power delivered by the generator is minimal, while the lamp continues to absorb $20\ \mathrm{W}$?
