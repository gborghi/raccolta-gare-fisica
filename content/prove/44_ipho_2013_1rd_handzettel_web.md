Problem 3 (14 points)
Climbing caterpillars
Two caterpillars each have
a mass of
10 g. One is 20 cm
long and thin, while the other caterpillar
is 10 cm long and thick.
The caterpillars crawl, as indicated in the figure, over
a thin edge 10 cm high.
Find out which of the caterpillars must expend more
energy to overcome the force of gravity while climbing up,
and determine the ratio of the energies the two caterpillars expend to overcome the force of gravity.
Junior problem (10 points)
Round 1
When? Registration is open from
April 2012. Submission deadlines can be found on
the IPhO website.
Who? Anyone interested who, in the school year
2012/2013, attends a general-education
German school and
was born after 30.06.1993.
Where? The problems are solved as
homework. You hand in your work to your
subject teacher for
correction.
How? Four problems are to be solved,
from all areas of physics. The
solutions may be written by hand or
on the computer
and should be comprehensible but not unnecessarily long.
Textbooks may be used provided the sources are cited. Formulas that appear in the common
textbooks need not be
derived. Only
individual work is permitted. Anyone who, in the
school year 2012/13, has not yet reached the
second-to-last grade level
can earn a points bonus with the junior problem.
What can you win?
All participants receive a
certificate of participation or an award certificate.
Round 2
When? September to October 2012.
Who? The problems are
published on the IPhO website at the end of August
and sent to all prizewinners of the first round.
Successful candidates from intermediate-level physics competitions or
Jugend Forscht may also
take part.
Where? You solve the problems again at
home and send your work
for correction by 30 October
2012, uncorrected, to your regional coordinator. It is later
reviewed once more at the IPN.
How? Theoretical and experimental physics problems are to be worked on. These are
more demanding than in the first
round. Otherwise the same
rules apply.
What can you win?
All participants receive an award certificate with an evaluation sheet. The
roughly 50 best are invited to the third
round.
The regional coordinators coordinate the running of the first two rounds in the individual federal states.
They are your contact persons up to the third round.
Round 3
When? 27 January - 02 February 2013.
Who? The roughly 50 best of the second round.
Where? The third round takes place as
a one-week seminar at DESY in
Hamburg.
How? The task now is to work on two theoretical
and
two experimental
exams without reference literature. In the afternoons,
seminars and excursions take place.
What can you win?
All participants receive, in addition to
a book voucher and a
subscription, an award certificate with an evaluation sheet. Young talents
are offered the opportunity to
take part in the European
Science Olympiad (EUSO), a
natural-science
team competition.
Round 4
When? April 2013.
Who? The 15 best of the third round.
Where? For the fourth round, the
participants are invited for a week to a
research centre.
How? Here, again, theoretical and experimental exams are on the programme. To
prepare for the IPhO,
problem seminars
are held
that are specifically geared toward typical IPhO questions.
What can you win?
The five most successful not only
make up the Olympic team but, with this round, also go through the selection process
for the German Academic Scholarship Foundation (Studienstiftung des deutschen
Volkes). For the others, besides a cash prize of 500 euros,
language trips and internships beckon. In addition, the Deutsche Physikalische Gesellschaft awards its student prize to the team members.
