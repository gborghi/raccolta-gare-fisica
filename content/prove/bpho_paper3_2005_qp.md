In this question you are asked to make reasoned estimates and assumptions. These must be clearly stated.

**a)**

**(i)** A large-print heading in the *National Geographic Magazine* reads: "Olivine is sharper than steel." Comment and explain. Note: olivine is a very hard glass-like volcanic rock.

**(ii)** A motorist gets his car stuck in a ditch (Figure 1.1). He is advised to attach a rope to a tree trunk, tighten it, and push the rope with a force $F$ at right angles to the rope. When the car has moved, he repeats the above procedure. Explain why this procedure is more effective than simply pulling the rope without bothering to use the tree trunk.

**(iii)** A razor, whether electric or manual, needs to be sharp in order to be comfortable when used. Why is this?

**(iv)** A stationary mass of $0.5\ \text{kg}$ is hit by a $2\ \text{kg}$ hammer moving at a speed of $1.3\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. If the collision is perfectly elastic, calculate the subsequent speed of the mass. Since the hardness of the hammer does not enter into the calculation, one might as well use a rubber hammer to drive in a nail. Comment.

**b)** Table 1 shows the timetable of a passenger boat on the river Rhine.

| Time | Town | Time | Town | Position along river / km |
|------|------|------|------|---------------------------|
| 1700 | St Goarhausen | 1851 | St Goarhausen | 642 |
| 1830 | Bacharach | 1801 | Bacharach | 659 |

Obtain a numerical estimate for the speed of the Rhine current and the speed of the boats in still water. N.B. the position marks follow the river Rhine, indicating the distance along its course from a zero mark.

**c)** It is desired to make an analogue electrical circuit that will replicate the behaviour of the mountain–lake–river system (Figure 1.2). Draw an electrical circuit analogue and explain how the circuit may be used to indicate the rate of rise of river level at point $A$ due to sudden rainfall on the high ground.

**d)** The distance from the centre of the Earth to the poles is $21\ \text{km}$ shorter than the radius of the equator. A one-second pendulum is taken from the equator (at sea level) to the North Pole. Make a rough estimate of its change in period. You may assume for the purposes of your calculation that the Earth has constant density.

**e)** When a perfect monatomic gas expands adiabatically (no heat interchange with its surroundings), the pressure and volume of the gas are related by:

$$P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma,$$

where in this case $\gamma = 5/3$, and $(P_1, V_1)$, $(P_2, V_2)$ refer to the initial and final states of the volume and pressure of the gas.

A helium balloon at atmospheric pressure is allowed to rise rapidly to a height such that the external pressure is half atmospheric pressure. The envelope of the balloon is at all times loose, of negligible mass, and regarded as a perfect heat insulator.

(i) Calculate the ratio of the initial to final volume of the balloon.
(ii) If the initial temperature of the gas was $300\ \text{K}$, what will be its new temperature?

**f)** During the 1998 International Olympiad in Iceland, competitors and leaders were surprised to see a cone of snow in the sea on the North West horizon. This was the mountain of Snaefell, which is about $2000\ \text{m}$ high. However, only the top $800\ \text{m}$ of the mountain could be seen. The mountain is about $120\ \text{km}$ from Reykjavik. Use these values to estimate the radius of the Earth.

**g)**

(i) How do tsunamis arise?
(ii) What is the order of magnitude of the wavelength, speed, and amplitude of the wave in the deep ocean?
(iii) How do these change when they approach land?

L'assorbimento della luce dovuto a questo stesso volume è $\alpha I_x\,\delta x$, dove $\alpha$ è una costante e $I_x$ è l'intensità incidente nella direzione di aumento $x$, normalmente incidentale sull'elemento di spessore $\delta x$. $$\frac{dI_x}{dx} = -\alpha I_x + k.$$
