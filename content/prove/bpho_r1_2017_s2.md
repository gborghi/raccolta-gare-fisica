**Question 2 — Electric current, resistance and non-linear components [25 marks]**

**a)** A 50 Hz alternating current of 10 A (rms) passes along a copper wire whose diameter is 1.0 mm. If copper contains $9.0\times10^{22}$ free electrons per cm$^3$, estimate
(i) the maximum speed of the electrons, and
(ii) the maximum displacement of the electrons associated with the current. **(6)**

**b)** A wire of length $l$ and resistance $R$ is extended by an amount $\varepsilon$ such that $\varepsilon\ll l$. If the volume of the wire and its resistivity remain unchanged,
(i) Give an expression for the fractional change in $R$, i.e. $\dfrac{\delta R}{R}$.
(ii) Calculate the new resistance of the wire if $R=100.0$ Ω and $\varepsilon/l=0.001$. **(6)**

**c)** A non-linear electrical component is connected in a circuit in series with a supply of emf $\varepsilon=300$ V and negligible internal resistance, and a resistor $R$ of 4.0 kΩ. The relation between the current $I$ through the non-linear component, and the potential difference across it, $V_c$, is given by $I=AV_c+BV_c^2$ where $A=0.070$ mA V$^{-1}$, $B=0.0050$ mA V$^{-2}$.
(i) Obtain an algebraic expression for $V_c$.
(ii) Determine the current $I$ through the non-linear component. **(6)**

**d)** A metal hemisphere of radius 0.10 m is immersed near the centre of a large conducting tank containing a liquid of resistivity 60 Ω m. The plane surface of the hemisphere is level with the surface of the liquid.
(i) Find an expression for the resistance between two hemispherical shells within the liquid, concentric with the centre of the metal hemisphere, having radii $r$ and $(r+\delta r)$ respectively.
(ii) Calculate the resistance between the hemisphere and the tank. **(7)**

**Risposta:** a) (i) $I_{max}=\sqrt2\times10=nAve$: $v_{max}=1.25\times10^{-3}$ m s$^{-1}$; (ii) $v_{max}=A\omega$, $A=\dfrac{v_{max}}{2\pi f}=3.98\times10^{-6}$ m. b) (i) $\dfrac{\delta R}{R}=\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{l}+\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{l^2}\approx\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{l}$; (ii) $\delta R=0.2$ Ω, $R'=100.2$ Ω. c) (i) $\varepsilon=IR+V_c$: $RBV_c^2+(RA+1)V_c-\varepsilon=0$, $V_c=\dfrac{-(RA+1)+\sqrt{(RA+1)^2+4RB\varepsilon}}{2RB}$; (ii) $V_c=94.6$ V, $I=51.4$ mA (directly in $I$: $I=51$ mA, $V_c=95$ V). d) (i) $\delta R=\dfrac{\rho\,\delta r}{2\pi r^2}$; (ii) $R=\dfrac{\rho}{2\pi r}=\dfrac{300}{\pi}=95.5=95$ Ω

**Problema 2 — Corrente elettrica, resistenza e componenti non lineari [25 punti]**

**a)** Una corrente alternata a 50 Hz di 10 A (efficaci) percorre un filo di rame di diametro 1,0 mm. Se il rame contiene $9{,}0\times10^{22}$ elettroni liberi per cm$^3$, stima
(i) la velocità massima degli elettroni e
(ii) lo spostamento massimo degli elettroni dovuto alla corrente. **(6)**

**b)** Un filo di lunghezza $l$ e resistenza $R$ viene allungato di un tratto $\varepsilon$ con $\varepsilon\ll l$. Se il volume del filo e la sua resistività restano invariati,
(i) scrivi un'espressione della variazione relativa di $R$, cioè $\dfrac{\delta R}{R}$;
(ii) calcola la nuova resistenza del filo se $R=100{,}0$ Ω e $\varepsilon/l=0{,}001$. **(6)**

**c)** Un componente elettrico non lineare è collegato in serie a un generatore di f.e.m. $\varepsilon=300$ V con resistenza interna trascurabile e a un resistore $R$ da 4,0 kΩ. La relazione tra la corrente $I$ nel componente non lineare e la differenza di potenziale ai suoi capi, $V_c$, è $I=AV_c+BV_c^2$, con $A=0{,}070$ mA V$^{-1}$, $B=0{,}0050$ mA V$^{-2}$.
(i) Ricava un'espressione algebrica di $V_c$.
(ii) Determina la corrente $I$ nel componente non lineare. **(6)**

**d)** Una semisfera metallica di raggio 0,10 m è immersa vicino al centro di una grande vasca conduttrice contenente un liquido di resistività 60 Ω m. La faccia piana della semisfera è a livello della superficie del liquido.
(i) Trova un'espressione della resistenza tra due gusci semisferici nel liquido, concentrici con il centro della semisfera metallica, di raggi $r$ e $(r+\delta r)$.
(ii) Calcola la resistenza tra la semisfera e la vasca. **(7)**

**Risposta:** a) (i) $I_{max}=\sqrt2\times10=nAve$: $v_{max}=1{,}25\times10^{-3}$ m s$^{-1}$; (ii) $v_{max}=A\omega$, $A=\dfrac{v_{max}}{2\pi f}=3{,}98\times10^{-6}$ m. b) (i) $\dfrac{\delta R}{R}=\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{l}+\dfrac{\varepsilon^2}{l^2}\approx\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{l}$; (ii) $\delta R=0{,}2$ Ω, $R'=100{,}2$ Ω. c) (i) $\varepsilon=IR+V_c$: $RBV_c^2+(RA+1)V_c-\varepsilon=0$, $V_c=\dfrac{-(RA+1)+\sqrt{(RA+1)^2+4RB\varepsilon}}{2RB}$; (ii) $V_c=94{,}6$ V, $I=51{,}4$ mA (risolvendo direttamente in $I$: $I=51$ mA, $V_c=95$ V). d) (i) $\delta R=\dfrac{\rho\,\delta r}{2\pi r^2}$; (ii) $R=\dfrac{\rho}{2\pi r}=\dfrac{300}{\pi}=95{,}5=95$ Ω

**Question 3 — A tug, a winch and a crane: dynamics and statics [25 marks]**

A tug boat winch is fitted with friction brakes that slip when the tension in the winch cable reaches 50 kN. The winch is mounted on a 450 tonne tug which is to tow a floating barge of mass 6300 tonne. The barge is initially at rest while the tug moves away from it at. The speed of the tug at the moment that the cable becomes taut is 2.5 m s$^{-1}$. At that moment the winch friction brakes start slipping in order to prevent the cable from breaking. The thrust exerted on the tug by its propeller is 35 kN. The winch and arrangement might look something like Figure 1a and Figure 1b:

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q03_fig1.png]]

*Figure 1*

**a)** Calculate
(i) The time during which the brakes are slipping,
(ii) The common velocity of the tug and the barge when the brakes stop slipping.
(iii) What length of cable runs out of the drum of the winch during the time the brakes are slipping? **(9)**

**b)** In a different geometry the winch is used to haul sand up from the bottom of a dry dock using a crane constructed from a very light framework of girders. The crane geometry can be seen in the diagram of Figure 2. The crane is fixed to the horizontal dock side at points A and B. The dimensions of the sides CB, CD and BD are as shown, the girder CB is vertical, and the winch is hauling a load of weight $15W$.
(i) Find the magnitude of the force exerted on D by the member BD,
(ii) The tension in the member CD.
(iii) If the force on the crane at A by the ground acts vertically downwards and has a magnitude $31W$, find the distance AB in terms of $L$.

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q03_fig2.png]]

*Figure 2* **(8)**

**c)** The bucket of mass $m$ is drawn up by the winch cable which exerts a steady force $F$ upwards, shown in Figure 3. The bucket starts from rest and initially contains a mass $m_0$ of sand. The sand leaks out at a constant rate so that the bucket is empty after time $t$, which occurs before it reaches the top of the dry dock wall.
In terms of $F$, $m_0$, $m$, $t$ and $g$ (the gravitational field strength), what is the velocity $v$ of the bucket when it is just empty? The friction brakes do not slip.

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q03_fig3.png]]

*Figure 3* **(8)**

You may use $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{(kx+a)}=\frac1k\ln(kx+a)+c$

**Risposta:** a) $a_b=\dfrac{50\times10^3}{6300\times10^3}=\dfrac{1}{126}$ m s$^{-2}$, $a_t=\dfrac{-15\times10^3}{450\times10^3}$ ($\tfrac{1}{30}$ m s$^{-2}$ deceleration); (i) $\tfrac{1}{126}t=2.5-\tfrac{1}{30}t$, $t=60.6$ s; (ii) $v=0.48$ m s$^{-1}$; (iii) $S=S_{tug}-S_{barge}=75.7$ m $=76$ m. b) $\cos\theta=0.8$: (i) $T_{BD}=24W$; (ii) $T_{CD}=15W$; (iii) moments about B, $31W\times AB=15W\times8L\sin\theta$, $AB=2.32L$. c) $v=\dfrac{t_fF}{m_0}\ln\left(1+\dfrac{m_0}{m}\right)-gt_f$ ($t_f$ = time for the sand to run out)

**b)** Consider the step index optical fibre shown in Figure 4.

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q04_fig1.png]]

*Figure 4*

The core, cladding and external medium have refractive indices $n_1$, $n_2$, and $n_3$, respectively. The acceptance angle $\theta$ for light entering the core of the fibre will allow transmission without loss providing the angle $\theta$ between the incident ray and the axis is smaller than the maximum acceptance angle $\theta_m$.
(i) Obtain an expression for $\sin\theta_m$ in terms of $n_1$, $n_2$, and $n_3$.

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q04_fig2.png]]

*Figure 5*

$$\frac1u+\frac1v=\frac1f$$
By taking the limit $u\to\infty$, explain what the length $f$ represents for a lens. **(2)**

**d)** Rays of light from a point source on the principal axis, passing through a converging lens by different paths and reaching a focus point on the principal axis, can be considered to do so by following paths which all have an equal time of travel. (This is a simplified form of a more general principle — Fermat's Principle of Least Time).
A flat, thin, circular disc of transparent material is made so that its refractive index $n$ varies with the distance $r$ from the principal axis normal to the centre of the disc, according to the equation $n=n_0(1-kr^2)$, where $n_0$ and $k$ are constant.
(i) By considering the times taken by light travelling by different paths (take a path not on the principal axis), show for rays travelling very close to the principal axis that the thin disc can behave as a lens. i.e. obtain an expression for $\dfrac1u+\dfrac1v$, if the thickness of the disc is $d$, with $d\ll u,v$.

**Problema 4 — Prismi, fibre ottiche e lente a indice variabile [25 punti]**

**a)** Calcola il più piccolo angolo di incidenza della luce sulla faccia di un prisma di 60° con indice di rifrazione 1,5 tale che tutta la luce che arriva sulla seconda faccia venga totalmente riflessa.
Disegna uno schema grande con il percorso della luce e gli angoli. **(5)**

**b)** Considera la fibra ottica a gradino d'indice di Figura 4.

![[BPhO_R1_2017_S2_Q04_fig1.png]]

*Figura 4*

Nucleo, mantello (cladding) e mezzo esterno hanno indici di rifrazione $n_1$, $n_2$ e $n_3$. La luce che entra nel nucleo della fibra si propaga senza perdite purché l'angolo $\theta$ tra il raggio incidente e l'asse sia minore dell'angolo massimo di accettazione $\theta_m$.
(i) Ricava un'espressione di $\sin\theta_m$ in funzione di $n_1$, $n_2$ e $n_3$.

**Question 5 — Electrostatics: charged pendulum, dipoles and a thundercloud [25 marks]**

**b)** Equal and opposite charges $+Q$ and $-Q$ are a distance of $2a$ apart. Taking the midpoint between them as the origin O, find the electric potential and the magnitude of the field strength, at a point P which is distance $r$ ($r\gg a$) from O, when

**c)** Equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance constitute a dipole. Calculate the work done needed to turn the molecule, which is lying parallel to a field of $3\times10^5$ V m$^{-1}$, through an angle of 180°. **(2)**

**d)** In a simple model of a thundercloud, an upper cylindrical shaped region (axis vertical) contains a net positve charge and the lower cylindrical shaped region of the cloud a net negative charge, as shown in Figure 6. The upper region, from 9 km to 12 km height, with a radius of 5 km has a charge density of 0.21 nC m$^{-3}$, whilst the lower region from 2 km to 9 km high, also with a radius of 5 km, has a charge density of $-0.18$ nC m$^{-3}$.

In order to determine the field strength at the ground due to these charges, we can aproximate these two volumes of charge as two point charges at the centres of each of the two regions (at heights 10.5 km and 5.5 km respectively). The ground is a good conducting plane surface and so charges will be induced in the surface. The effect of this induced charge can be simulated by considering a charge of equal magnitude but opposite sign at the same distance $h$ below the surface as the real charge at height $h$ above the surface. The field at the surface is then calculated from the field of the real charge at the surface added to the field due to the image charge at the surface.

(ii) For the two charges calculated, with their two image charges of opposite signs, calculate the strength of the electric field at the surface of the Earth directly below the thundercloud.

**Question 6 — The Doppler effect: boats, sound, the Sun and a binary star [25 marks]**

**a)** Sea waves of frequency $f$ are moving North with velocity $c$. A small boat moves South with a velocity $u$, both velocities being measured with respect to the land. By counting the number of waves moving past the boat in a time $\Delta t$ or otherwise, show that the boat will be moving up and down with a frequency $f'$ given by
$$f'=\frac{(u+c)f}{c}$$ **(4)**

**b)** (i) A source of sound, emitting a single note of frequency $f$ is moving towards a stationary observer with speed $v_s$. Show that if the air is stationary, the frequency $f'$ detected by the observer is given by
$$f'=\frac{f}{\left(1-\frac{v_s}{c}\right)}$$
where $c$ is the speed of sound in air. You may wish to consider a time $\Delta t$, and how many waves are emitted by the source, how far the waves and the source each move in this time, the wavelength the observer then detects, or any other approach.
(ii) What would be the value of $\Delta f=f'-f$ if the source is moving away from the observer at a speed $v_s=c/2$?
(iii) If $v_s\ll c$, show that $\dfrac{\Delta f}{f}\approx\dfrac{v_s}{c}$ **(6)**

**c)** The above formula can be applied to electromagnetic waves.
(i) Show that the observed frequency of an absorption line of emitted frequency $f$ in the spectrum of the Sun should vary between $f+\delta f$ and $f-\delta f$ where
$$\delta f=\frac{2\pi Rf}{Tc}$$
in which $R$ is the radius of the Sun, and $T$ the period of rotation.
(ii) Given that the density of the Sun is $1.4\times10^3$ kg m$^{-3}$, the mass is $2.0\times10^{30}$ kg and the equatorial rotation period is 24.5 days, estimate the fractional frequency shift, $\dfrac{\delta f}{f}$, for the peak in the visible spectrum at 550 nm. **(5)**

**d)** Two stars labelled A and B separated by a distance $a=19.8$ AU form a binary pair, gravitationally bound, orbiting a common centre of mass with a period $T=50.1$ y. Star A, with mass $m_A$ is at radius $R_A$ from the centre of mass, and star B, a very faint white dwarf, of mass $m_B$ orbits at radius $R_B$. The stars have circular orbits with the plane of the orbit lying along the line of sight from the Earth. Only the brightest of the two stars can be observed through a telescope, and it is found that a spectral line of 656.3 nm in the lab varies by $\pm0.0235$ nm when observed in the bright star.
(i) Show that $T^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3$ where $M=m_A+m_B$ is the total mass of the binary pair.
(ii) From the figures given, calculate a value for the total mass of the system.
(iii) Show that $v_A=\dfrac{GT}{2\pi a^2}m_B$ where $v_A$ is the orbital velocity of star A.
(iv) From the spectral line shift, calculate mass $m_B$ and hence the mass of star A, $m_A$.
(v) Hence calculate the ratio of the orbital radii, $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}$.
1 AU $=1.50\times10^{11}$ m **(10)**

**Risposta:** a) $f'\Delta t=f\Delta t+u\Delta t/\lambda$ with $f\lambda=c$: $f'=f(1+u/c)=f(u+c)/c$ (any clear method; no mark for the given answer). b) (i) $f\Delta t$ waves fit in $(c-v_s)\Delta t$, $\lambda'=(c-v_s)/f=c/f'$, $f'=f/(1-v_s/c)$; (ii) $f'=\dfrac{f}{1+\frac12}=\tfrac23f$, $\Delta f=-\tfrac13f$; (iii) $f'\approx f(1+v_s/c)$, $\Delta f/f\approx v_s/c$. c) (i) $v=2\pi R/T$, $\delta f/f\approx v/c$, $\delta f=\dfrac{2\pi Rf}{Tc}$; (ii) $M/\rho=\tfrac43\pi R^3$, $R=699\times10^6$ m, $\delta f/f=6.9\times10^{-6}$. d) (ii) $M=\dfrac{4\pi^2a^3}{GT^2}=6.21\times10^{30}$ kg; (iii) $m_Av_A^2/R_A=Gm_Am_B/a^2$ with $v_A=2\pi R_A/T$; (iv) $m_B=\dfrac{2\pi a^2c\,\Delta f}{fGT}=5.65\times10^{30}$ kg, $m_A=5.6\times10^{29}$ kg; (v) $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}=\dfrac{m_B}{m_A}=10.(1)$

(v) Calcola infine il rapporto tra i raggi orbitali $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}$.
1 UA $=1{,}50\times10^{11}$ m **(10)**

**Risposta:** a) $f'\Delta t=f\Delta t+u\Delta t/\lambda$ con $f\lambda=c$: $f'=f(1+u/c)=f(u+c)/c$ (qualunque metodo chiaro; nessun punto per il solo risultato dato). b) (i) $f\Delta t$ onde occupano $(c-v_s)\Delta t$, $\lambda'=(c-v_s)/f=c/f'$, $f'=f/(1-v_s/c)$; (ii) $f'=\dfrac{f}{1+\frac12}=\tfrac23f$, $\Delta f=-\tfrac13f$; (iii) $f'\approx f(1+v_s/c)$, $\Delta f/f\approx v_s/c$. c) (i) $v=2\pi R/T$, $\delta f/f\approx v/c$, $\delta f=\dfrac{2\pi Rf}{Tc}$; (ii) $M/\rho=\tfrac43\pi R^3$, $R=699\times10^6$ m, $\delta f/f=6{,}9\times10^{-6}$. d) (ii) $M=\dfrac{4\pi^2a^3}{GT^2}=6{,}21\times10^{30}$ kg; (iii) $m_Av_A^2/R_A=Gm_Am_B/a^2$ con $v_A=2\pi R_A/T$; (iv) $m_B=\dfrac{2\pi a^2c\,\Delta f}{fGT}=5{,}65\times10^{30}$ kg, $m_A=5{,}6\times10^{29}$ kg; (v) $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}=\dfrac{m_B}{m_A}=10(,1)$
