Problem 1 (10 points)
A Round Matter
Determine the following quantities and give them rounded to whole numbers.
a) The ratio of the airspeed of an aircraft in still air to the wind speed in the following case: for one leg the
aircraft needs 7.0 hours flying against the wind, while it covers the same distance on the return leg with the wind in 6.0 hours.
b) The acceleration (in multiples of g = 9.81 ms-2) of a wet sock in a washing-machine drum with a diameter of 40 cm in
the gentle spin cycle at 600 revolutions per minute.
c) The smallest resistance value (in $\Omega$) that can be achieved by combining the following resistors without short-circuiting them:
one resistor each of $100\Omega$, $220\Omega$, $330\Omega$ and 360 $\Omega$.
d) The mass of ice (in kg) that warms from an initial -10 $^{\circ}C$ to 10 $^{\circ}C$ by the supply of a quantity of heat of 2.4 MJ.
Strung together correctly and completed with the right power of ten and unit, the numbers represent the digits of a natural constant. State this
natural constant together with its name.

Problem 4 (10 points)
Tasty Lens
The aim of this problem is the experimental determination of the refractive index of jelly,
also called gelatin dessert. To do this, obtain a jelly mix for stirring up and
pour the still-liquid jelly mass into a cylindrical, transparent
vessel to set. Alternatively, you can also buy a ready-made jelly in a transparent and
preferably cylindrical container. The jelly in the vessel forms a cylindrical
lens. If you let the light of a light source fall onto the lens from the side, you can
produce an image of the light source on a screen on the other side.
Using the jelly lens, experimentally determine the refractive index of the
jelly. Carry out your measurements several times and estimate the uncertainty of your
result.
Hints: It is helpful to choose as symmetric an arrangement of the ray path as possible,
which allows the use of the lensmaker's equation for thin lenses. In the
case where the thickness of the vessel wall is small compared with the diameter of the lens, you can
assume that the vessel is part of the lens.

Problema 4 (10 punti)
Lente Gustosa

Lo scopo di questo problema è la determinazione sperimentale dell'indice di rifrazione della gelatina, comunemente nota come dessert in gelatina. A tale scopo, ottenete una miscela di gelatina da preparare e versate la massa di gelatina ancora liquida in un recipiente cilindrico trasparente per farla indurire. In alternativa, potete acquistare una gelatina già pronta in un contenitore trasparente e preferibilmente cilindrico. La gelatina nel recipiente forma una lente cilindrica. Se fate incidere la luce di una sorgente luminosa sulla lente da un lato, potete ottenere un'immagine della sorgente luminosa su uno schermo dall'altra parte.

Utilizzando la lente di gelatina, determinate sperimentalmente l'indice di rifrazione della gelatina. Eseguite diverse misurazioni e stimatene l'incertezza.

Consigli: È utile scegliere un'impiego del cammino ottico il più simmetrico possibile, che consenta l'utilizzo dell'equazione del costruttore della lente per lenti sottili. Problem 4 (10 points)
Tasty Lens

The goal of this problem is the experimental determination of the refractive index of jelly, also known as gelatin dessert. To achieve this, obtain a jelly mix for preparation and pour the still-liquid jelly into a cylindrical, transparent container to let it set. Alternatively, you may purchase a ready-made jelly that is in a transparent and preferably cylindrical container. The set jelly inside the vessel forms a cylindrical lens. When light from a light source is directed onto this lens from the side, an image of the light source can be formed on a screen located on the opposite side.

Using the jelly lens, experimentally determine the refractive index of the jelly. Perform your measurements multiple times and estimate the uncertainty in your result.

Hints: It is advantageous to set up the optical arrangement as symmetrically as possible, which allows application of the lensmaker’s equation for thin lenses. In cases where the wall thickness of the container is small compared to the lens diameter, you may assume that the container walls are part of the lens.
