Long problem 2.2: Liquid crystals (16 points)

Some airplane windows can adjust their transparency using liquid crystal technology, which controls how much light passes through. This is performed by adjusting the polarization of the throughgoing light. Polarization denotes the oscillation direction of the electric field of the light (which is an electromagnetic wave). The polarization is always perpendicular to the propagation direction. If light with intensity I goes through a linear polarizer, the transmitted light intensity It​ is generally given by It​=Icos2(α)(1) where α is the angle between the polarization direction of the incoming light and the transmission axis of the second polarizer.

Part A. System Sketch

First, light passes through a static linear polarizer. Then, the light passes through a liquid crystal layer that changes the polarization of the light by an angle Δφ. In its ground state (no field), the liquid crystal rotates the polarization by an angle Δφ0​. When an electric field is applied, the rotation angle decreases. Finally, light passes through a second linear polarizer placed at a distance d and aligned at an angle θ=90∘ with respect to the first.

i. (2 pts) Sketch the system, label the distance between the filters and insert a label for the intensity at each stage.

ii. (1 pt) Describe in your own words the polarization state of the incoming light.

iii. (1 pt) Describe in your own words the polarization state of the outgoing light.

Part B. Capacitor Behavior

The liquid crystal layer has a thickness of d=5μm, and a maximum applied voltage of Vmax​=12V. The relative permittivity of the liquid crystal is εr​=10.

i. (2 pts) Calculate the electric field strength Emax​ between the two polarizers across the liquid crystal layer when the maximum voltage is applied.

ii. (1 pt) Determine the capacity C of a 0.125m2 area of the liquid crystal layer.

Part C. Adjusting the Intensity

The angle α is determined by: α=θ−Δφ(C.1) where the polarization change Δφ depends on the applied voltage: Δφ=Δφ0​(1−Vmax​V​)(C.2) where Vmax​=12V completely eliminates the polarization rotation.

i. (2 pts) What is the light intensity ILC​ after the first linear polarizer with respect to the intensity of the incoming light I0​?

ii. (1 pt) You want to have a window with maximum transparency. If you are free to choose the value of Δφ0​∈(0,2π), what value will you set it to?

iii. (3 pts) Calculate the voltage V1​ that must be applied to reduce the transmitted light intensity by 70% with respect to the incoming light.

Part D. Calculator Screens

The same technology is used in displays of many calculators. The only conceptual difference is that a mirror is placed at one side of the setup. Light thus traverses the system twice.

i. (2 pts) In this case, what voltage V2​ has to be applied to reduce the intensity by 70% with respect to the incoming light?

ii. (1 pt) What is the value of the fraction V2​V1​​?
