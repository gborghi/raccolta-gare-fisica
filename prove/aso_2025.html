Sliding Snails

Imagine a human walking on perfectly flat ground. With each step they accelerate and decelerate.

1a) Which force is responsible for this acceleration and deceleration?

The underneath of a snail is called a foot. When a snail is backlit and travels across a surface, light regions called ‘waves’ and dark regions called interwaves can be observed in the snail’s foot moving from its tail to its head. The rim refers to the outer part of the snail’s foot. See the diagram below.

The snail is made up of segments separated by thin lines. These can be seen in the diagram below. In the diagram a white line highlights one of these thin lines between segments.

Soluzione:

1a) Friction.

1b) In order to propel itself forward the snail must apply a backwards force on the glass. This must occur at a point which is moving backwards relative to the snail (the interwave). To avoid slipping (like a human on ice) there must be traction/no relative motion between the interwave and the glass. Since segments of the snail that are in wave and rim sections are moving relative to the interwave sections these cannot be stationary relative to the glass.

Practicals with Paper

This exam booklet is (hopefully) made of A4 paper. Typical office paper has a thickness defined by 80 grams per square meter. An A4 sheet of paper has a defined size and is part of the A-series of paper, which also includes A3 or A5 paper. These paper size regulations are defined by the ISO 216 standard. The A-series of paper has a few intriguing properties:

  • Every size in the series has the same ratio of long edge to short edge.
  • If you join two sheets of the same size along the long edge, you get the paper that is the next up in the series. For example, two A4 pieces of paper joined along the long edge gives an A3 page.
  • A0 paper has an area of exactly 1 square meter.

2a) Calculate the length and width of an A4 piece of paper to 4 significant figures.

2b) The mass of a typical A4 sheet of paper is 5×10a kg. What is the integer exponent a?

2c) Estimate the density of the paper comprising this exam booklet. Briefly explain your method and calculations, and include an estimate of the uncertainty in your measurement.

When two surfaces move past each other while in contact, they experience a friction force that acts to oppose the motion. The magnitude of the frictional force between two surfaces sliding past one another is given by F=μN(1) where N is the normal force between the two surfaces and μ is the coefficient of kinetic friction, a measure of ‘roughness’ between the two surfaces.

Fun with Friction

Friction forces oppose the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. The maximum force due to friction depends on the normal force N between the two surfaces (i.e. how strongly they are pressed together) and also the coefficient of friction (which depends on what the surfaces are made of). The maximum force due to friction is given by F=μN(1)

A cylinder of height h and radius r is placed upright on a ramp that is inclined at angle θ to the horizontal. This means that the circular face of the cylinder is against the ramp. The cylinder is of uniform density and has mass M. The coefficient of friction between the cylinder and the ramp is μ.

3a) Draw a free body diagram of the cylinder (that is, a diagram showing all of the forces acting on the cylinder).

3b) Stephen slowly tilts the ramp to increase its steepness. As the angle θ increases, eventually the cylinder will tip over. What is the angle of the ramp when the cylinder begins to tip over? Assume that the coefficient of friction between the ramp and cylinder is large, so that the cylinder does not slide on the ramp.

Next we will consider what happens if the coefficient of friction μ is lower, so that the cylinder slides down the ramp instead of tipping.

3c) The cylinder begins at a distance L from the bottom of the ramp. Find an equation for the amount of time, t, that it takes for the cylinder to slide to the bottom of the ramp.

3d) Find a numerical value for t (in seconds) for the case where L=4 m, h=20 cm, r=10 cm, M=1 kg, θ=30∘ and μ=0.37. Write your answer to three significant figures.

Now consider a ramp made from two different materials. The left side of the ramp has friction coefficient μL​ and the right side of the ramp has friction coefficient μR​. The boundary between the two halves of the ramp is perfectly straight and in the centre of the ramp. The cylinder is initially placed in the centre of the ramp, so the centre of mass is over the boundary between the two surfaces and half of the cylinder is on each side. Then, it is released and begins to slide down the ramp.

3e) Suppose that μL​<μR​. As the cylinder slides down the ramp, does its centre of mass move towards the left side of the ramp, the right side of the ramp, or remain in the centre? Explain your thinking using words (and diagrams if you wish).

Risposta: b) tanθ=2r/h; c) t=g(sinθ−μcosθ)2L​​; d) t=2.13 s; e) thin-disk model: stays on the centre line; tall cylinder: moves left (lower friction side)

Soluzione:

3a) All three forces in the correct directions: gravitational force (the largest) straight down; normal force at 90∘ to the slope; friction force (the smallest) up the slope.

3b) The problem can be approached geometrically by finding the angle at which the centre of mass is directly above the edge of the cylinder: tanθ=h2r​. Alternatively, when the cylinder is about to tip over the normal force from the ramp must act at the lower edge of the cylinder; set the sum of torques about any point on the cylinder to zero.

3c) Sum of forces parallel to the ramp surface: ma=mgsinθ−μmgcosθ. From kinematics s=ut+21​at2, so L=21​at2. Therefore t=g(sinθ−μcosθ)2L​​.

3d) t=2.13 s.

3e) Two distinct cases can be considered; full marks are given for either explanation if it is correct.

Case 1 – thin disk approximation (the pressure on each part of the disk is even). The friction force always opposes the relative motion of the surfaces. Since the friction on one side of the cylinder is greater, there is a net torque on the cylinder from the ramp and so it begins to rotate. Adding the friction forces on every point of the contact surface, the left–right components on each (left–right) half of the cylinder cancel out (the top always cancels out the bottom half). So the net friction force is directed up the ramp (not side-to-side) and the centre of mass remains on the centre line between the two sides.

Case 2 – tall cylinder regime (students must explicitly state that the pressure on the front of the cylinder is higher than on the back). The object still rotates, but now the forces on the front (bottom) half of the cylinder do not cancel those on the back (top) half. Viewed from above, the cylinder rotates anticlockwise; therefore it experiences a net force to the left and moves towards the left (lower friction) side.

La forza d’attrito massima dipende dalla forza normale N tra le due superfici (cioè da quanto sono premute l’una contro l’altra) e dal coefficiente d’attrito (che dipende dai materiali delle superfici). Introduction to Curved Spacetime

When a physical situation has spherical symmetry, it becomes more convenient to use a spherical polar coordinate system instead of a normal cartesian (x,y,z) coordinate system. The diagram below illustrates a polar coordinate system where any point in 3D space can be represented by three coordinates:

  • a distance from the origin (r);
  • an angle from the north pole (θ), called the polar angle;
  • a longitude angle moving towards the y-axis from the x-axis (ϕ), called the azimuthal angle.

Note, the angular coordinates are given in radians. Also note that in this question d is not a variable, it refers to an infinitesimal (tiny) change in a variable. So in the diagram above, dr means a tiny change in radius and dθ and dϕ mean tiny changes in angle.

4a) Consider two different objects. Object 1 is originally at the coordinate (r1​,θ1​,ϕ1​). Object 2 has the coordinates (r2​,θ1​,ϕ1​), where r2​>r1​. Imagine that both objects are rotated around the vertical axis by the same angle. This results in a small change in ϕ1​. Which object travels further? Justify your answer.

In physics, the term metric refers to a formula that defines how distances are measured. In cartesian coordinates the metric is given by Pythagoras’s theorem: (ds)2=(dx)2+(dy)2+(dz)2(1) where ds is the distance between two points and dx, dy and dz are the differences in the x, y and z coordinates respectively. When using spherical polar coordinates the metric is given by (ds)2=(dr)2+(rdθ)2+(rsinθdϕ)2(2) Note that this formula is only accurate for small changes in coordinates dr, dθ and dϕ.

4b) Suppose that r and θ are kept constant (dr=0 and dθ=0). In this circumstance how does (ds)2 depend on r, θ and dϕ? Explore what the equation predicts and explain whether the behaviour makes sense or not.

The theory of relativity proposed by Einstein between 1905 and 1915 states that time and space are not separate but should be considered together as a four-dimensional spacetime. The spacetime around a non-rotating mass can be described by the Schwarzschild metric. In this case, the spacetime interval (the spacetime equivalent of distance) between two nearby events (events have a time coordinate t as well as position coordinates) is (ds)2=−(cdt1−rc22GM​​)2+(1−rc22GM​​dr​)2+(rdθ)2+(rsinθdϕ)2(3) where (ds)2 is the spacetime interval between two events and dt is a small time interval between the two events as observed by someone far away from the mass. θ and ϕ are defined as in spherical polar coordinates. However, the r coordinate is now defined differently because it may not be possible to walk radially out from a heavy mass: we construct a giant spherical frame around the mass, and define the coordinate r for any point on the frame as the circumference of the frame divided by 2π. G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the heavy object and c is the speed of light.

4c) At what points do singularities (both coordinate and physical) occur in the spacetime coordinate system defined by equation 3?

Imagine that we created two spherical frames: frame 1 has radius r=r1​ and frame 2 has radius r=r2​, where r1​>r2​>c22GM​. Imagine you are on the inside of frame 1 and extend a tape measure directly towards the centre of the frame until it touches frame 2. In everyday life, you would expect to measure a distance of r1​−r2​.

4d) If frames 1 and 2 were built around a very large mass, would you actually measure r1​−r2​ as the distance between the frames, or would you measure a different distance? Explain what you would measure and justify your answer.

We now define r1​=r2​+Δr, such that frame 1 always has an r-coordinate slightly greater than frame 2. We now use a (perfectly rigid) tape measure to measure the distance between the two frames. If you set t=0, θ=π/2 and ϕ=0, this distance is given by ΔL2=1−r1​c22GM​Δr2​.

4e) Plot a graph of Δr2ΔL2​ against r1​. Label any singularities on your diagram.

When two events occur at the same position, the proper time between the events is defined as the time that passed for an observer who was present at both events and is given by dτ=−(ds)2​. This time may not be the same as the time observed by someone far away, who would measure the coordinate time dt.

4f) Imagine you are at a safe distance (r≫2GM/c2) and you have a strobe light which is falling and getting close to a heavy mass in the centre of the frame. The light is programmed to flash once per second. Describe what you would see watching this light if you remain at the safe distance. Justify your answer.

4g) How would this compare with your observations if you were to fall with the strobe light towards the centre?

Risposta: a) object 2; c) r=2GM/c2, r=0, θ=0, θ=π, ϕ=0, ϕ=2π; d) a distance larger than r1​−r2​; f) flashes become less and less frequent; g) once per second

Soluzione:

4a) The further away you are from the origin, the more distance is swept out for the same angle. Therefore, as r2​>r1​, object 2 travels a larger distance.

4b) As r gets larger, ds gets larger. If r=0 the distance travelled is 0 for any change in ϕ. Similarly, if sinθ=0 (at θ=0 or θ=π, the north and south poles) the distance travelled is 0 regardless of the change in ϕ. For a given dϕ, ds increases as θ increases up to a maximum at θ=π/2 and then decreases again. dϕ is meant to be a small change; for large dϕ the maximum would occur at dϕ=π and the distance would go to zero as dϕ→0 and dϕ→2π.

At the North Pole, ϕ can change even though nothing moves. That is not a problem with the place, it is a problem with the coordinates: a coordinate singularity. The maths looks weird, but space itself is normal. This is different from a physical singularity, where spacetime itself breaks down.

4c) r=c22GM​, r=0, θ=0, θ=π, ϕ=0 and ϕ=2π.

4d) You would measure a distance larger than r1​−r2​, because of the factor 1/1−rc22GM​​, which is strictly greater than 1, multiplying dr. No integration of the dr term is expected; only a qualitative explanation is required.

4e)

The curve diverges at r1​=2GM/c2 and tends to the asymptote Δr2ΔL2​=1 for large r1​.

4f) An external observer sees the flashes becoming less and less frequent: because of the scale factor on the time coordinate, one must wait longer and longer before the same spacetime interval occurs between consecutive flashes. (Some students considered the Doppler effect; this also gives the correct answer.)

4g) If you fall with the strobe light, you observe the flashes occurring at a constant rate of once per second.

A coordinate singularity occurs at the Schwarzschild radius, the point of no return around a black hole: at this boundary nothing can escape outward, not even light. The coordinates used in the standard black hole solution become confusing there but, just like at the North Pole, that does not mean the place itself is broken.

Introduzione allo spaziotempo curvo

Quando una situazione fisica ha simmetria sferica conviene usare un sistema di coordinate polari sferiche invece delle normali coordinate cartesiane (x,y,z). Il diagramma seguente illustra un sistema di coordinate polari, in cui ogni punto dello spazio 3D è rappresentato da tre coordinate:

  • la distanza dall’origine (r);
  • l’angolo dal polo nord (θ), detto angolo polare;
  • l’angolo di longitudine, misurato dall’asse x verso l’asse y (ϕ), detto angolo azimutale.

Le coordinate angolari sono espresse in radianti. In questo quesito d non è una variabile, ma indica una variazione infinitesima (piccolissima) di una variabile: nel diagramma dr è una piccolissima variazione del raggio e dθ, dϕ sono piccolissime variazioni degli angoli.

4a) Considera due oggetti. L’oggetto 1 si trova inizialmente nel punto di coordinate (r1​,θ1​,ϕ1​), l’oggetto 2 in (r2​,θ1​,ϕ1​), con r2​>r1​. Immagina di ruotare entrambi gli oggetti dello stesso angolo attorno all’asse verticale, con una piccola variazione di ϕ1​. Quale oggetto percorre la distanza maggiore? Giustifica la risposta.

In fisica il termine metrica indica la formula che definisce come si misurano le distanze. In coordinate cartesiane la metrica è data dal teorema di Pitagora: (ds)2=(dx)2+(dy)2+(dz)2(1) dove ds è la distanza tra due punti e dx, dy, dz sono le differenze delle coordinate x, y e z. In coordinate polari sferiche la metrica è (ds)2=(dr)2+(rdθ)2+(rsinθdϕ)2(2) Questa formula vale solo per piccole variazioni delle coordinate dr, dθ e dϕ.

4b) Supponi che r e θ restino costanti (dr=0 e dθ=0). In questo caso, come dipende (ds)2 da r, θ e dϕ? Esplora che cosa prevede l’equazione e spiega se il comportamento ha senso oppure no.

La teoria della relatività, proposta da Einstein tra il 1905 e il 1915, afferma che tempo e spazio non sono separati ma vanno considerati insieme come uno spaziotempo quadridimensionale. Lo spaziotempo attorno a una massa non rotante è descritto dalla metrica di Schwarzschild. In questo caso l’intervallo spaziotemporale (l’equivalente spaziotemporale della distanza) tra due eventi vicini (gli eventi hanno una coordinata temporale t oltre a quelle spaziali) è (ds)2=−(cdt1−rc22GM​​)2+(1−rc22GM​​dr​)2+(rdθ)2+(rsinθdϕ)2(3) dove (ds)2 è l’intervallo spaziotemporale tra i due eventi e dt è un piccolo intervallo di tempo tra i due eventi misurato da un osservatore lontano dalla massa. θ e ϕ sono definiti come nelle coordinate polari sferiche. La coordinata r invece è definita diversamente, perché potrebbe non essere possibile allontanarsi radialmente da una grande massa: si costruisce un’enorme struttura sferica attorno alla massa e si definisce la coordinata r di ogni punto della struttura come la circonferenza della struttura divisa per 2π. G è la costante gravitazionale, M la massa del corpo e c la velocità della luce.

4c) In quali punti si hanno singolarità (sia di coordinate sia fisiche) nel sistema di coordinate spaziotemporali definito dall’equazione 3?

Immagina di costruire due strutture sferiche: la struttura 1 ha raggio r=r1​ e la struttura 2 ha raggio r=r2​, con r1​>r2​>c22GM​. Immagina di trovarti all’interno della struttura 1 e di stendere un metro a nastro dritto verso il centro fino a toccare la struttura 2. Nella vita di tutti i giorni ti aspetteresti di misurare una distanza r1​−r2​.

4d) Se le strutture 1 e 2 fossero costruite attorno a una massa molto grande, misureresti davvero r1​−r2​ come distanza tra le strutture, o una distanza diversa? Spiega che cosa misureresti e giustifica la risposta.

Definiamo ora r1​=r2​+Δr, così che la struttura 1 abbia sempre una coordinata r di poco maggiore della struttura 2, e usiamo un metro a nastro (perfettamente rigido) per misurare la distanza tra le due strutture. Ponendo t=0, θ=π/2 e ϕ=0, questa distanza è ΔL2=1−r1​c22GM​Δr2​.

4e) Traccia il grafico di Δr2ΔL2​ in funzione di r1​, indicando sul diagramma le eventuali singolarità.

Quando due eventi avvengono nella stessa posizione, il tempo proprio tra i due eventi è il tempo trascorso per un osservatore presente a entrambi gli eventi ed è dato da dτ=−(ds)2​. Questo tempo può non coincidere con quello osservato da qualcuno lontano, che misurerebbe il tempo coordinato dt.

4f) Immagina di trovarti a distanza di sicurezza (r≫2GM/c2) e di avere una luce stroboscopica che cade avvicinandosi a una grande massa al centro della struttura. La luce è programmata per lampeggiare una volta al secondo. Descrivi che cosa vedresti osservando la luce restando a distanza di sicurezza. Giustifica la risposta.

4g) Come cambierebbero le tue osservazioni se cadessi verso il centro insieme alla luce stroboscopica?

Risposta: a) l’oggetto 2; c) r=2GM/c2, r=0, θ=0, θ=π, ϕ=0, ϕ=2π; d) una distanza maggiore di r1​−r2​; f) lampi sempre più radi; g) un lampo al secondo
