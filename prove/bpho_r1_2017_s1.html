a) Physicists sometimes use the approximation that light travels in a vacuum at a speed of 1 foot in 1 ns. What is the percentage error in using this value? (1.000 m = 1.094 yards and 1.000 yard = 3.000 feet) [3]

Risposta: 1 foot/ns =3.047×108 m s−1; error =3.0003.047−3.000​=1.564%=1.6% (using 1−0.9846=1.54% gains only 2/3 marks)

c) A particle moves in a straight line with an intial acceleration of 10 m s−2. The acceleration decreases uniformly with time until, after ten seconds, the acceleration is 5 m s−2, and from then on the acceleration remains constant. If the intial velocity is 100 m s−1, (i) find when the velocity has doubled; (ii) sketch a graph of the velocity against time. [7]

Risposta: (i) in the first 10 s average acceleration 7.5 m s−2, so v=175 m s−1 at 10 s; the extra 25 m s−1 at 5 m s−2 takes 5 s: t=15 s after the start; (ii) curve up to 10 s, then linear, no kink (values not required)
