Question 2 — Electric current, resistance and non-linear components [25 marks]

a) A 50 Hz alternating current of 10 A (rms) passes along a copper wire whose diameter is 1.0 mm. If copper contains 9.0×1022 free electrons per cm3, estimate (i) the maximum speed of the electrons, and (ii) the maximum displacement of the electrons associated with the current. (6)

b) A wire of length l and resistance R is extended by an amount ε such that ε≪l. If the volume of the wire and its resistivity remain unchanged, (i) Give an expression for the fractional change in R, i.e. RδR​. (ii) Calculate the new resistance of the wire if R=100.0 Ω and ε/l=0.001. (6)

c) A non-linear electrical component is connected in a circuit in series with a supply of emf ε=300 V and negligible internal resistance, and a resistor R of 4.0 kΩ. The relation between the current I through the non-linear component, and the potential difference across it, Vc​, is given by I=AVc​+BVc2​ where A=0.070 mA V−1, B=0.0050 mA V−2. (i) Obtain an algebraic expression for Vc​. (ii) Determine the current I through the non-linear component. (6)

d) A metal hemisphere of radius 0.10 m is immersed near the centre of a large conducting tank containing a liquid of resistivity 60 Ω m. The plane surface of the hemisphere is level with the surface of the liquid. (i) Find an expression for the resistance between two hemispherical shells within the liquid, concentric with the centre of the metal hemisphere, having radii r and (r+δr) respectively. (ii) Calculate the resistance between the hemisphere and the tank. (7)

Risposta: a) (i) Imax​=2​×10=nAve: vmax​=1.25×10−3 m s−1; (ii) vmax​=Aω, A=2πfvmax​​=3.98×10−6 m. b) (i) RδR​=l2ε​+l2ε2​≈l2ε​; (ii) δR=0.2 Ω, R′=100.2 Ω. c) (i) ε=IR+Vc​: RBVc2​+(RA+1)Vc​−ε=0, Vc​=2RB−(RA+1)+(RA+1)2+4RBε​​; (ii) Vc​=94.6 V, I=51.4 mA (directly in I: I=51 mA, Vc​=95 V). d) (i) δR=2πr2ρδr​; (ii) R=2πrρ​=π300​=95.5=95 Ω

Problema 2 — Corrente elettrica, resistenza e componenti non lineari [25 punti]

a) Una corrente alternata a 50 Hz di 10 A (efficaci) percorre un filo di rame di diametro 1,0 mm. Se il rame contiene 9,0×1022 elettroni liberi per cm3, stima (i) la velocità massima degli elettroni e (ii) lo spostamento massimo degli elettroni dovuto alla corrente. (6)

b) Un filo di lunghezza l e resistenza R viene allungato di un tratto ε con ε≪l. Se il volume del filo e la sua resistività restano invariati, (i) scrivi un’espressione della variazione relativa di R, cioè RδR​; (ii) calcola la nuova resistenza del filo se R=100,0 Ω e ε/l=0,001. (6)

c) Un componente elettrico non lineare è collegato in serie a un generatore di f.e.m. ε=300 V con resistenza interna trascurabile e a un resistore R da 4,0 kΩ. La relazione tra la corrente I nel componente non lineare e la differenza di potenziale ai suoi capi, Vc​, è I=AVc​+BVc2​, con A=0,070 mA V−1, B=0,0050 mA V−2. (i) Ricava un’espressione algebrica di Vc​. (ii) Determina la corrente I nel componente non lineare. (6)

d) Una semisfera metallica di raggio 0,10 m è immersa vicino al centro di una grande vasca conduttrice contenente un liquido di resistività 60 Ω m. La faccia piana della semisfera è a livello della superficie del liquido. (i) Trova un’espressione della resistenza tra due gusci semisferici nel liquido, concentrici con il centro della semisfera metallica, di raggi r e (r+δr). (ii) Calcola la resistenza tra la semisfera e la vasca. (7)

Risposta: a) (i) Imax​=2​×10=nAve: vmax​=1,25×10−3 m s−1; (ii) vmax​=Aω, A=2πfvmax​​=3,98×10−6 m. b) (i) RδR​=l2ε​+l2ε2​≈l2ε​; (ii) δR=0,2 Ω, R′=100,2 Ω. c) (i) ε=IR+Vc​: RBVc2​+(RA+1)Vc​−ε=0, Vc​=2RB−(RA+1)+(RA+1)2+4RBε​​; (ii) Vc​=94,6 V, I=51,4 mA (risolvendo direttamente in I: I=51 mA, Vc​=95 V). d) (i) δR=2πr2ρδr​; (ii) R=2πrρ​=π300​=95,5=95 Ω

b) Equal and opposite charges +Q and −Q are a distance of 2a apart. Taking the midpoint between them as the origin O, find the electric potential and the magnitude of the field strength, at a point P which is distance r (r≫a) from O, when (i) P is on the line passing through the charges; (ii) When P is on a perpendicular bisector of the line joining the charges. (10)

c) Equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance constitute a dipole. A hydrogen chloride molecule may be treated as a dipole in which an electron is separated from a positive charge of equal magnitude by a distance of 2×10−11 m. Calculate the work done needed to turn the molecule, which is lying parallel to a field of 3×105 V m−1, through an angle of 180°. (2)

d) In a simple model of a thundercloud, an upper cylindrical shaped region (axis vertical) contains a net positve charge and the lower cylindrical shaped region of the cloud a net negative charge, as shown in Figure 6. The upper region, from 9 km to 12 km height, with a radius of 5 km has a charge density of 0.21 nC m−3, whilst the lower region from 2 km to 9 km high, also with a radius of 5 km, has a charge density of −0.18 nC m−3.

Figure 6: ref. Mazur V, LH Runhke: JGR, v103, D18, pp23299-23308, 1998

(i) Calculate the magnitudes of the positive and negative charges in each region.

In order to determine the field strength at the ground due to these charges, we can aproximate these two volumes of charge as two point charges at the centres of each of the two regions (at heights 10.5 km and 5.5 km respectively). The ground is a good conducting plane surface and so charges will be induced in the surface. The effect of this induced charge can be simulated by considering a charge of equal magnitude but opposite sign at the same distance h below the surface as the real charge at height h above the surface. The field at the surface is then calculated from the field of the real charge at the surface added to the field due to the image charge at the surface.

(ii) For the two charges calculated, with their two image charges of opposite signs, calculate the strength of the electric field at the surface of the Earth directly below the thundercloud. (iii) If a two metre tall person stands with his feet on the ground in this region, what is the magnitude and polarity of the potential at his head? Take the potential of the ground to be 0 V. (7)

Risposta: a) (i) tanθ=mgQE​; (ii) (mg−QE)θ=−mlθ¨, T=2πmg−QEml​​; (iii) QE<mg. b) (i) V≈−4πε0​1​r22Qa​ (either sign is OK), E≈4πε0​1​r34Qa​ (only the magnitude is required); (ii) V=0, E≈4πε0​r32Qa​ to lowest order. c) W=2QEd=1.9(2)×10−24 J. d) (i) Qu​=49.5 C, QL​=(−)99.0 C; (ii) Es​=4πε0​1​(ru2​2Qu​​−rL2​2QL​​)=(−)50.8 kV m−1; (iii) the potential at the head is −102 kV (the answer may also be given as: head more positive than the feet, the person being a conductor)

Question 6 — The Doppler effect: boats, sound, the Sun and a binary star [25 marks]

a) Sea waves of frequency f are moving North with velocity c. A small boat moves South with a velocity u, both velocities being measured with respect to the land. By counting the number of waves moving past the boat in a time Δt or otherwise, show that the boat will be moving up and down with a frequency f′ given by f′=c(u+c)f​ (4)

b) (i) A source of sound, emitting a single note of frequency f is moving towards a stationary observer with speed vs​. Show that if the air is stationary, the frequency f′ detected by the observer is given by f′=(1−cvs​​)f​ where c is the speed of sound in air. You may wish to consider a time Δt, and how many waves are emitted by the source, how far the waves and the source each move in this time, the wavelength the observer then detects, or any other approach. (ii) What would be the value of Δf=f′−f if the source is moving away from the observer at a speed vs​=c/2? (iii) If vs​≪c, show that fΔf​≈cvs​​ (6)

c) The above formula can be applied to electromagnetic waves. (i) Show that the observed frequency of an absorption line of emitted frequency f in the spectrum of the Sun should vary between f+δf and f−δf where δf=Tc2πRf​ in which R is the radius of the Sun, and T the period of rotation. (ii) Given that the density of the Sun is 1.4×103 kg m−3, the mass is 2.0×1030 kg and the equatorial rotation period is 24.5 days, estimate the fractional frequency shift, fδf​, for the peak in the visible spectrum at 550 nm. (5)

d) Two stars labelled A and B separated by a distance a=19.8 AU form a binary pair, gravitationally bound, orbiting a common centre of mass with a period T=50.1 y. Star A, with mass mA​ is at radius RA​ from the centre of mass, and star B, a very faint white dwarf, of mass mB​ orbits at radius RB​. The stars have circular orbits with the plane of the orbit lying along the line of sight from the Earth. Only the brightest of the two stars can be observed through a telescope, and it is found that a spectral line of 656.3 nm in the lab varies by ±0.0235 nm when observed in the bright star. (i) Show that T2=GM4π2​a3 where M=mA​+mB​ is the total mass of the binary pair. (ii) From the figures given, calculate a value for the total mass of the system. (iii) Show that vA​=2πa2GT​mB​ where vA​ is the orbital velocity of star A. (iv) From the spectral line shift, calculate mass mB​ and hence the mass of star A, mA​. (v) Hence calculate the ratio of the orbital radii, RB​RA​​. 1 AU =1.50×1011 m (10)

Risposta: a) f′Δt=fΔt+uΔt/λ with fλ=c: f′=f(1+u/c)=f(u+c)/c (any clear method; no mark for the given answer). b) (i) fΔt waves fit in (c−vs​)Δt, λ′=(c−vs​)/f=c/f′, f′=f/(1−vs​/c); (ii) f′=1+21​f​=32​f, Δf=−31​f; (iii) f′≈f(1+vs​/c), Δf/f≈vs​/c. c) (i) v=2πR/T, δf/f≈v/c, δf=Tc2πRf​; (ii) M/ρ=34​πR3, R=699×106 m, δf/f=6.9×10−6. d) (ii) M=GT24π2a3​=6.21×1030 kg; (iii) mA​vA2​/RA​=GmA​mB​/a2 with vA​=2πRA​/T; (iv) mB​=fGT2πa2cΔf​=5.65×1030 kg, mA​=5.6×1029 kg; (v) RB​RA​​=mA​mB​​=10.(1)
