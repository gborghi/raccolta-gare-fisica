Question 2 — Forces on dam walls and ultrasonic crack detection [25 marks]

This is about forces on dam walls and detecting cracks in them through wave reflection.

a) The dam shown in Fig. 1 is used to generate hydroelectric power. The difference in height between the water levels of the dam is 61 m, and the peak flow rate of the water through the dam is 3380 m3 s−1. Estimate the maximum power output of the hydroelectric facility. (3)

Figure 1: HEP dam (credit: Wikimedia Commons, EPA archives, The Dalles Dam).

b) In practice not all of the gravitational potential energy of the water behind the dam is transferred by the generators, and the water emerges from the dam with a velocity of 8.0 m s−1. Calculate the efficiency of the energy transfer process from the water. (3)

c) The dam can be considered to be a concrete prism of triangular cross-section, with height h=60 m, a base of thickness t=60 m, and a width of w=2700 m. The cross-section of the dam is illustrated in Figure 2. Points F and C are at the bottom of the dam. The water level is at the top of the dam wall.

Figure 2

(i) Calculate the resultant force on the face of the dam due to water pressure in terms of h, w, ρ and g, where ρ is the density of water.

(ii) Calculate the resultant moment (torque) about F due to this force, and calculate the effective height at which the resultant force acts to produce this moment.

The centre of mass of a uniform triangle is at the point of intersection of its medians (the lines joining its vertices to the midpoints of the opposite sides). Thus the centre of mass lies two-thirds of the way along each median.

(iii) For the dam, state the position of the centre of mass of the dam relative to F.

(iv) Calculate the two turning moments acting on the dam about C, due to the water pressure and the weight of the dam.

(v) The frictional force of the dam on the ground beneath is given by f=μN where N is the normal reaction force of the ground, and μ=0.75. Calculate the minimum density of concrete that could be used so that the dam will not slide over the ground.

(vi) Calculate the minimum density of concrete used to construct the dam so that it will not tip up about point C.

d) There is a significant danger of cracks forming in dams, leading to catastrophic failure with little or no warning. One form of non-destructive testing uses ultrasonic waves to investigate the interior of the concrete. When a wave strikes a boundary (interface) between two different media it has to obey two constraints.

  • the displacement of the material by the wave must be the same either side of the boundary.
  • the energy transferred into the boundary must be the same as the total energy reflected and transmitted.

If an incident wave has an amplitude Ai​, the reflected wave amplitude Ar​ and the transmitted wave amplitude At​, then we can write: Ai​+Ar​=At​(1) The energy of a wave is proportional to the square of the amplitude, A2, and the rate of energy flow in a medium is given by ΔtΔE​=ω2ZA2, where Z stands for the acoustic impedance and is a parameter of the medium, and ω is a constant (it cancels in all our equations and can be ignored).

(i) From the energy flow through a boundary from medium 1 to medium 2, show that Z1​Ai2​−Z1​Ar2​=Z2​At2​(2) where Z1​ and Z2​ are the impedances of the media either side of the boundary, as illustrated in Figure 3.

Figure 3

Using these two requirements (eq. (1) and (2)) for a wave crossing an interface between two media at normal incidence:

(ii) show that Ai​Ar​​=Z1​+Z2​Z1​−Z2​​, and

(iii) derive an expression for Ai​At​​ in terms of Z1​ and Z2​.

(iv) If Z1​=Z2​, what can be said about the transmitted and reflected waves and the values of Ai​Ar​​ and Ai​At​​?

(v) When Z2​>Z1​, what effect does this produce?

The energy reflected back can be used to characterise cracks in the concrete. The coefficient of reflection, R, is the ratio of the reflected to the incident energy flow, such that R=Ai2​Ar2​​. The coefficient of transmission, T, is the ratio of the transmitted energy flow in medium 2 to the incident energy flow in medium 1.

(v) Obtain expressions for R and T in terms of Z1​ and Z2​.

(vi) Calculate a value for R+T.

Problema 2 — Forze sulle dighe e ricerca di crepe con gli ultrasuoni [25 punti]

Il problema riguarda le forze sulle dighe e la ricerca di crepe al loro interno mediante la riflessione delle onde.

a) La diga di Fig. 1 serve a produrre energia idroelettrica. Il dislivello tra i due specchi d’acqua della diga è 61 m e la portata massima dell’acqua attraverso la diga è 3380 m3 s−1. Stima la potenza massima erogata dalla centrale idroelettrica. (3)

c) La diga si può considerare un prisma di calcestruzzo a sezione triangolare, di altezza h=60 m, base di spessore t=60 m e larghezza w=2700 m. La sezione della diga è mostrata in Figura 2. I punti F e C sono alla base della diga. Il livello dell’acqua arriva in cima alla parete della diga.

Figura 2

(ii) Calcola il momento risultante rispetto a F dovuto a questa forza e l’altezza efficace alla quale la forza risultante agisce producendo tale momento.

Il centro di massa di un triangolo omogeneo è nel punto d’incontro delle mediane (i segmenti che congiungono i vertici con i punti medi dei lati opposti); si trova quindi a due terzi di ciascuna mediana.

(iii) Indica la posizione del centro di massa della diga rispetto a F.

(iv) Calcola i due momenti che agiscono sulla diga rispetto a C, dovuti alla pressione dell’acqua e al peso della diga.

(v) La forza d’attrito tra la diga e il terreno sottostante è f=μN, dove N è la reazione normale del terreno e μ=0,75. Calcola la densità minima del calcestruzzo perché la diga non scivoli sul terreno.

(vi) Calcola la densità minima del calcestruzzo perché la diga non si ribalti attorno al punto C.

Densità dell’acqua ρw​=1000 kg m−3 (10)

d) Nelle dighe c’è un serio pericolo che si formino crepe, che possono portare a un cedimento catastrofico con poco o nessun preavviso. Un metodo di controllo non distruttivo usa onde ultrasoniche per esplorare l’interno del calcestruzzo. Quando un’onda incontra la superficie di separazione (interfaccia) tra due mezzi diversi deve rispettare due vincoli.

  • lo spostamento del materiale prodotto dall’onda deve essere lo stesso ai due lati dell’interfaccia;
  • l’energia che arriva sull’interfaccia deve essere uguale all’energia totale riflessa e trasmessa.

Se l’onda incidente ha ampiezza Ai​, l’onda riflessa Ar​ e l’onda trasmessa At​, possiamo scrivere: Ai​+Ar​=At​(1) L’energia di un’onda è proporzionale al quadrato dell’ampiezza, A2, e il flusso di energia in un mezzo è ΔtΔE​=ω2ZA2, dove Z è l’impedenza acustica, un parametro del mezzo, e ω è una costante (si semplifica in tutte le equazioni e si può ignorare).

(i) Dal flusso di energia attraverso un’interfaccia dal mezzo 1 al mezzo 2, mostra che Z1​Ai2​−Z1​Ar2​=Z2​At2​(2) dove Z1​ e Z2​ sono le impedenze dei mezzi ai due lati dell’interfaccia, come in Figura 3.

Figura 3

(ii) mostra che Ai​Ar​​=Z1​+Z2​Z1​−Z2​​, e

(iii) ricava un’espressione di Ai​At​​ in funzione di Z1​ e Z2​.

(iv) Se Z1​=Z2​, che cosa si può dire delle onde trasmessa e riflessa e dei valori di Ai​Ar​​ e Ai​At​​?

(v) Che effetto si ha quando Z2​>Z1​?

L’energia riflessa si può usare per caratterizzare le crepe nel calcestruzzo. Il coefficiente di riflessione R è il rapporto tra il flusso di energia riflesso e quello incidente, R=Ai2​Ar2​​. Il coefficiente di trasmissione T è il rapporto tra il flusso di energia trasmesso nel mezzo 2 e quello incidente nel mezzo 1.

(v) Ricava le espressioni di R e T in funzione di Z1​ e Z2​.

(vi) Calcola il valore di R+T.

Per un mezzo, Z=ρvsound​, dove ρ è la densità del mezzo e vsound​ la velocità del suono nel mezzo. Densità e velocità del suono in aria, calcestruzzo e acqua sono riportate in Tabella 1.

This question is on gravity and forces.

a) In 2017 an object that became known as Oumuamua was observed in the Solar System. Figure 13 shows an artist’s impression.

Figure 13 (Picture: ESO)

b) In January 2019 the New Horizons spacecraft made a fly by of a distant object in the Solar System called Ultima Thule, shown in Figure 14 below. The planetoid consists of two lobes of diameters 2r1​=19.5 km and 2r2​=14.2 km. The period of rotation about its short axis is T=15 hours. (The long axis is a line passing through the centres of the two lobes, and a short axis is perpendicular to the long axis.)

Figure 14: The two approximately spherical lobes making up Ultima Thule. Credits: NASA/Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory/Southwest Research Institute, National Optical Astronomy Observatory

If we assume that the two lobes are held together by a weak gravitational attraction, we can model the object as two spheres of the same density just touching.

(i) Find the centre of mass of Ultima Thule, as measured from the centre of the largest sphere.

(ii) By considering the resultant force acting on one sphere, estimate the density of Ultima Thule.

(iii) Using the fact that the density can be taken to be similar for the two lobes, sketch a graph of the gravitational field strength along the long axis. Note: the field strength varies linearly with radius inside a spherical body of uniform density. You may find it helpful to sketch the potential along the long axis. (8)

In fact, the two lobes are not bound by gravity, so the density and the masses of the lobes are not known. From the appearance of the two lobes, it looks as though they were moving at a low relative speed when they collided. As an example, consider two spherical iron masses with the same radii as the lobes of Ultima Thule. The masses are m1​=3.06×1016 kg and m2​=1.18×1016 kg for the larger and smaller lobes respectively. We can assume that they approached from a great distance apart along a line through their centres, with a negligible initial relative velocity.

(iv) Calculate the amount of energy liberated when these two iron spheres collided.

(v) Calculate the relative speed of approach. (7)

This energy of collision may have initially led to an increase in temperature of the two spheres (you may neglect any energy of rotation), which we could calculate from knowing the specific heat capacity of iron. However, at the very low temperatures of the outer Solar System the molar heat capacity of a material is not constant. For example, at low temperatures the molar heat capacity of iron, c, varies as c=5θD3​12π4R​T3 where:

  • c is the molar heat capacity in J mol−1 K−1
  • R is the universal gas constant, R=8.31 J mol−1 K−1
  • θD​ is a constant, θD​=464 K for iron
  • T is the temperature in kelvin
  • molar mass of iron = 56 g mol−1

(vi) Calculate the number of moles of iron involved in the collision.

(vii) Using your value of the energy liberated on collision, calculate the maximum temperature reached by the iron spheres, assuming the initial temperature was 4 K and radiative losses are in the first instance negligible. (6)
